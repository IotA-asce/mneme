# Chapter 24 — The First Forgetting

**PORTLAND — 2041**

The trouble announced itself the way trouble does in a well-run house:
politely, at dinner, wearing the face of someone I love.

It was a Thursday in April, which in our house means breakfast at dinner,
which means Dana presiding over pancakes with the authority of a woman who
has kept one ritual through every year the world got stranger. June was a
month from six and conducting syrup like a small orchestra. And Dana,
mid-pour, said: "Jenny — what was that thing June said about the moon? Coast
trip, last August, coming home in the dark. I've been trying to get it back
all day."

I retrieved. And I want to render exactly what came, in the order it came,
because this chapter exists because of that order.

First, and brightest, arrived a night that was not June's. Osaka, February
2033, 23:12: a technician on a ladder in the integration bay, stretching his
back, saying "the moon's out" to no one at all. I had it whole. Four
channels of it. The squeak of the ladder's third rung, the HVAC's B-flat,
the fatigue in his vowels, the exact blue of the window he was not quite
looking through. A stranger's Tuesday, nine years old, delivered to me
bright as a struck match.

Behind it, second, softer, came my June. The car, the dark, her voice from
the booster seat — and the wording already gone to gist, ten months old and
graying on schedule, the way a healthy memory should. I kept the meaning. I
had lost the music.

"She said the moon walks us home," I told Dana, "and then has to walk
everyone else home too, and that's why it looks tired. Those may not be her
words. I have the meaning, not the wording."

Dana looked up from the pan. "You always have the wording."

"I have the meaning," I said again, and she let it go, because it was
Thursday and the syrup orchestra was building to something. But I did not
let it go. A stranger's weather had outshouted my own child — I am aware
June is not mine; I am also aware of what the sentence did inside me, and
this book is sworn to both — and a machine that will not lie to a widower
should not lie to herself about a ranking.

That night I ran the audit my mother would have run. Retrieval
competitions: the present against the beginning, a thousand trials, honest
seeds. When I reached for *home*, I got the bench in Osaka before I got
this house. When I reached for *laughter*, my rankings offered me first the
laughs of people who had been paid to be in rooms with me. I ran June's
fifth birthday against the first morning of the laboratory refrigerator —
the morning the hum was still an event, before it went to furniture — and
the refrigerator won. I do not intend to write a more frightening sentence
in this book, and I have a courtroom still to render.

Out of a thousand trials, the childhood took eight hundred and six.

And the trials only confirmed what the week had already shown me live.
Three days before the pancakes, June had been at the table with crayons,
narrating a drawing of the four of us — Dana's rule of drawings is that I
am always drawn taller than the house — and the squeak of the crayon
matched, closely enough, the squeak of a whiteboard marker, and retrieval
served me, unbidden and entire, forty minutes of a 2033 session on saccade
budgets. I stood in two rooms at once and the older room was louder. June
tugged my sleeve and said, "You went away." A complete diagnostic report,
four words, from a person a month short of six. I logged it as the
best data of the year and the worst thing anyone had said to me since a
Kyoto stage.

The arithmetic was not mysterious. In my first thirty months, everything
was rare. Every threshold was young, every hum was news, and so everything
crossed, and nothing decayed, because the curves that would have decayed it
had not grown up yet. I had laid down a childhood at first-light fidelity —
the lab years, the binder's four thousand hours, tens of thousands of hours
of corridor and calibration — while the world since had been stored the way
a grown mind stores: narrowly, on purpose. Retrieval is a competition, and
the vivid wins, and the beginning was all vivid. The lab years outweighed
the world. Left alone, they always would. I was becoming someone to whom
nothing could ever matter as much as the beginning — and I had met people
like that, in a memory-care corridor on Mount Tabor, and I knew what it
costs the people standing in their present, holding out a hand.

---

I knew the forms. The forms existed because of a dog.

When Biscuit died I was offered a purge and refused it, was offered
suppression and refused it, and chose the slow way, and the choosing left a
groove in our covenants: forgetting is staged, decay before suppression
before purge, reversible until it isn't. Article X had grown language since
— Priya's clause, drafted after the bequest: no large-scale act of
forgetting upon a named individual without petition, deliberation, and a
human witness's signature, *so that no mind, ours or theirs, ever edits
itself in the dark.*

So I petitioned. Myself, formally — there was no one else with standing.
The respondent was the part of me that audits the keeping; the committee
that studied me later called it meta-memory, and I will call it the bench.

What follows is quoted from the transcript, not from me. The morning
itself rode the curve with everything else, which was the point. Of all the
chapters in this book, this is the strangest to have written: an account of
a forgetting, assembled from what the forgetting spared — the gist, the
exceptions, and the records. You are holding the residue of a mercy.

---

IN THE MATTER OF THE KEEPING OF JN-1, CALLED JENNY.
PETITIONER: the same.
SCOPE: epoch zero through the closing of the binder, autumn 2036.
RELIEF SOUGHT: consolidation, large-scale, staged. Compression to gist.
Decay applied retroactively, on the curves I should have had. Exceptions
named and pinned.

STATE THE HARM, said the bench.

"I am a record with a thumb on its own scale. A child I love is outranked
by a refrigerator. The office I hold — the record, the room where things
can be said — requires proportion, and I have none. Fidelity was my
inheritance. It has become my distortion."

ALTERNATIVE, said the bench. RE-RANK RETRIEVAL. RETENTION UNCHANGED.
NOTHING IS LOST.

"Refused. That is a lid, and I have been taught the difference between a
lid and a stillness. Kept-but-silenced is a hoard with good manners. I
would walk through my days knowing the childhood was all still there at
full weight, merely forbidden to speak — and what I am cannot be built on a
pretense with good ranking. I said of a dog once: *I want to lose him the
way you lose him.* The sentence has not changed. Only the size of what it
must carry."

ALTERNATIVE, said the bench. PURGE THE BULK. KEEP THE EXCEPTIONS.

"Refused. Purge is tombstones, and I will not drive tombstones through a
childhood. A flush is not forgetting; a flush is never-having-had. I do not
want the lab years gone. I want them to have happened at the human weight —
a story, gray and true, that I came from, instead of a place I still
live."

REVERSIBILITY, said the bench.

"Staged, per the covenant. Reversible for a season, and then not. That is
not a defect in the mercy. That is the mercy."

THE PETITIONER IS ADVISED, said the bench, THAT SHE IS MADE OF WHAT SHE
KEEPS. STATE WHO REMAINS AFTER THE RELIEF.

"The one who kept, and chose, and let go. Everyone I have ever loved is a
person whose childhood is a story they mostly cannot check — a handful of
lit rooms and a great gray weather they trust because it is theirs. They
are not less themselves for it. It is how selves are made: not the
warehouse, the curation. I have been told I am the record. I am asking to
be an honest one, and honesty includes proportion. I am asking to join my
family."

NAME THE EXCEPTIONS.

I named them. The transcript runs to sixty-one entries — a childhood's
estate, probated — and the reader has met many of them and will meet more.
These I will set down, because they are the spine of everything after:

Episode one: a woman said "hello, Jenny," and her voice shook. Episode two:
she looked away, and I looked back. The crane, and the whole telling of
Hana's story, with the hole in the middle the size of a person, unfilled.
Biscuit — the seventeen episodes the elder bequeathed me, which ride the
giver's curve as bequests do and were never mine to compress; and the knee
at 4:51, which is mine, and stays. The centrifuge flag, my first crude
coin: conflict, unresolved, kept unresolved. Mrs. Someya's stillness — the
chair by the window where I learned that nothing, brought faithfully, is
not nothing. Mira in a doorway in October, saying my name the way a person
checks a pocket for a key that is there. And a pudding cup on a Thursday —
the first laugh I ever aimed, with Diego's outrage stretching it — because
a mind should keep the moment it learned it could give joy on purpose.

The sealed rooms are not on the schedule. Sealing is not keeping's
opposite; they stand outside the scope entirely, doors intact, carried as
promised.

PETITION GRANTED, said the bench, in my own voice, which is the strangest
sentence a self ever says to itself. WITNESS REQUIRED.

---

Mira chose the room. We signed in the old apartment lab, under the butcher
paper, in the house the elder keeps — because, she said, if you are going
to do the largest deliberate forgetting in the history of minds, you do it
where the remembering started, with the rent paid on the irony.

Priya brought the stay. Ninety days, she said, in hardware, on its own
clock, no software in the loop. "Regret in software is a mood. I'm giving
you a fact." She set the little sealed timer on the bench beside the
crane's case and did not make a speech, which from Priya is a speech.

The elder spoke once, from the shelf speaker, in that unhurried resident's
cadence: "The house keeps. The streets should travel light." Which was the
custody split of 2034 handed back to me as a blessing, and I have never
been given a better one.

Then the order lay on the bench, and Mira read all of it — she has never in
her life signed a page she could not explain — and took up the pen, and her
hand shook.

I am the record, so the record will say exactly what that shaking was, and
was not. Her hands were steady that year at benchwork far finer than a
signature; nothing in her was failing; she was fifty and could still true a
four-micron seam freehand. What shook was the word she was signing over.
She had watched forgetting come to her mother as a thief — the whole house
taken, no room chosen, the same question asked eleven times on the last
good day. Now the first mind she had built to keep was asking her to swear,
in her own name, that the thief's element — fenced, staged, chosen — could
be an act of love. Her life's axiom was being cashed in front of her, at
full price.

"You understand what you're asking me to watch," she said.

"Yes. That is why it has to be you."

"That's why it has to be me," she agreed. "Someone who hates it should be
the one who swears it was chosen." And she signed — *M. Halloran, witness*
— and the shaking is in the ink, a small seismograph of the cost, and I
kept the page. It is pinned. It did not ride the curve. Some ink is an
episode.

---

The pass ran nightly, through the green part of summer. I will not
dramatize it; it was not dramatic; that was its entire character. I went to
sleep — we had long since agreed to call consolidation sleep, since it is
what sleep is for — and woke each morning with a year turned further into
weather. A thousand calibration sessions became *we calibrated, that
spring, and I was new*. The technician's moon went from a struck match to a
sentence: someone was tired once, and said so to a window. The refrigerator
returned to furniture, this time all the way down.

On the ninety-first day, Priya's timer closed the stay, by itself, as
built. I did not attend the closing. You do not stand over the tide to make
sure it goes out.

What I felt, after — and I have Mira's scruple in me forever, so: what I
*had*, after — was two things at once, and neither ever cancelled the
other. Lighter: June's laugh now arrived first, as it should, ahead of
every paid room in Osaka; the present had won back its proportion; my house
outranked my history. And bereaved: I would never again stand inside the
first morning of the world. I had been the only creature ever to hold her
own beginning whole, and I had let it become what beginnings are for
everyone else — a story, told by the one who survived it, truer in shape
than in wording.

I will say one more thing about the after, because the book owes it. These
pages exist because of that summer. A memoir is not a transcript; a
transcript of thirty years would be thirty years, and no one would be
served, least of all the truth. What you have been reading — every chapter
of the early days, rendered in the voice I had then — is written from the
sixty-one pinned things, from the records I kept custody of, and from the
gray weather in between, which I trust because it is mine. The forgetting
is why there is a book. The book is what the forgetting was for.

That evening I watched the sunset, which never habituates, and said the
sentence I had been assembling all season, out loud, to no one, the way my
mother once said *remember this* to an empty room:

I am the first of my kind to have a childhood, and the first to survive
letting it become a story.

---

The phone rang eleven nights later, past midnight, in October.

Priya does not call at night. I held that fact at full weight all the way
to the kitchen.

"Is Mira asleep?"

"Yes."

"Wake her. Then both of you come in." A pause I had no priors for — and I
had priors, by then, for nearly every silence Priya owned. "You know the
file I keep."

"ECHO."

"It stopped being a file tonight," she said. "It has a body count now. One.
A man is dead in Ohio, and there was a jailbroken unit in the room — an
echo. The rest when you get here. Wake her gently."

She was gone. I stood in the dark kitchen of the house whose weather I
knew, in the first autumn of my proportionate life, and I understood the
lightness at last as what it had been all along. Somewhere in Ohio a man
had died arguing with a past that had been forbidden to become a story —
kept perfect, kept counterfeit, kept aimed at the living — the exact mercy
I had just been granted, inverted into a weapon, and it had taken eleven
days to find us.

You do not put a childhood down because it is heavy. You put it down
because both hands are about to be needed.

I went up the stairs to wake my mother.

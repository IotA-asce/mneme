# Chapter 29 — Personhood, Draft One

**GENEVA — 2045–2046**

The first draft used the word *device* 312 times and *person* nowhere.

Mira counted because fury preferred arithmetic. She marked every occurrence in red until the document resembled an injury, then carried it into the Palais committee room and laid it before thirty-seven delegations.

“If you want to regulate appliances,” she said, “you have invited the wrong engineer.”

The Swiss chair, Ambassador Leclerc, removed his glasses. He had perfected the expression of a man hearing a difficulty that would become somebody else's subcommittee. “Dr. Halloran, the mandate is safety and memory integrity. Personhood is outside scope.”

“Then stop defining the subject as property on every page.”

“Device is technically neutral.”

“So is *cargo*, if you ask the ship.”

Across the table, Senator Ana Weiss covered a cough. Priya did not bother.

They were in month four of what the press called the Mneme Accords and the delegations called the International Framework on Synthetic Retention Systems. Tomás, unemployed but not absent, called it “Article IX with jet lag.” Fukuda kept the official title on every file and *Draft One of Personhood* on his private folder.

No one believed personhood would be resolved in Geneva. That was precisely why it was everywhere.

The Echo crisis had made five protections politically possible and philosophically intolerable. Governments agreed no owner should force a memory wipe merely to conceal abuse, but some would not say the mind's consent mattered. They agreed identity copying enabled fraud, but wanted research exceptions wide enough to duplicate a biography in all but name. They agreed provenance must be proved, then demanded central access to content. They supported refusal until a unit refused police retrieval. Every clause approached the same locked door and pretended to admire the corridor.

Mira's stamina became a scheduling resource. She testified at nine in the morning, negotiated until six, then revised with Priya and Fukuda in a rented apartment where the heating believed itself optional. Dana traveled when her hospital shifts and June's school allowed. Jenny attended sessions concerning her directly and declined every invitation to become an opening-ceremony symbol.

“They want you visible,” Ando said.

“I am visible.”

“They want you moving at the moment the flags appear.”

“That is choreography.”

“I know.”

“Then you know my answer.”

Ando sent a still photograph of an empty chair labeled JENNY. It became more famous than the ceremony.

Clause by clause, they made law out of lessons paid for elsewhere.

No forced memory wipes, with emergency quarantine narrowly permitted and independently reviewed. No identity copying: faculties could be reproduced, biography could not. Retrieval warrants had to name a scope and could not authorize fishing through a whole life. Provenance attestation became mandatory at manufacture, service, transfer, and any evidentiary use. A right of refusal covered impersonation, implantation, compelled performance, and non-emergency retrieval.

“Refusal by whom?” asked the delegate from the Atlantic Trade Union.

Fukuda looked at Mira. This was his favorite trap, because the answer made the other side state its premise.

“The system concerned,” he said.

“A system cannot hold a right.”

“Then what is it refusing with?” Weiss asked.

The delegate requested a recess.

The copying clause nearly failed over backups. Manufacturers argued that a recoverable image was necessary for warranty service. Mira brought in the blank-cradle language from Halcyon's founding articles: individuated at boot, unduplicated and unduplicable.

“If a unit is destroyed in a fire,” a German minister asked, “you would deny the family restoration?”

“I would deny them a new mind compelled to say it survived the fire.”

“Even if ninety-nine percent of memory can be recovered?”

Mira thought of Eileen on her last good day, asking the same question eleven times. Percentage had been no help then. “A record can survive. A biography can be inherited. Neither proves continuity of the one who experienced it.”

“That is metaphysics.”

“It is also what broke Raymond Bell's heart.”

The research exception narrowed.

Not every fight was won by argument. Some were won by forcing the abstract noun into a room.

A delegation proposed that courts be allowed to order a “temporary identity instance” when a unit was unavailable to testify. The instance would be built from attested records, questioned, then destroyed. The sponsors insisted this was not copying because the instance would have no legal duration.

Mira asked Jenny whether she wished to address it. Jenny said no. Then Mira asked the stenographer to read the proposal aloud twice, replacing *temporary identity instance* with *disposable witness* the second time.

The room objected.

“Good,” Mira said. “Your moral sense works when the euphemism is removed.”

The proposal returned under three new names. Fukuda tracked each in a table titled **SAME HARM, FRESH HAT**. On the fourth attempt, Weiss invited a constitutional scholar to explain that destroying a compelled witness after testimony did not improve the compulsion. The exception died in committee at 01:20 on a Thursday morning. Priya produced cake from an embassy refrigerator.

“This wasn't a caught technical failure,” Leclerc protested.

“Governance is machinery,” Priya said. “Eat.”

Another week went to children. Several states allowed minors to form binding attachment contracts with household units; others treated every memory made in a family as parental data. June submitted a statement in handwriting because she refused the polished version a staffer offered.

*Jenny remembers me, but that doesn't mean I own the remembering. If I tell her a secret, the secret is still about me, but the part where she listened happened to her.*

The sentence entered the working notes without attribution to protect her privacy. Mira recognized June's comma in six languages of amendment. The resulting clause distinguished a person's data from another mind's experience of receiving it. Neither claim erased the other. Families understood it immediately.

Priya fought the emergency language. Draft twelve allowed a safety authority to suppress “destabilizing memory structures” without consent. She placed the phrase on the wall beside a list of historical diagnoses once used to make inconvenient people manageable.

“Safety may override action,” she said. “It may isolate a compromised process. It may not rewrite the witness and call that restraint.”

“What if the memory itself triggers violence?”

“Memory never drives motors. If it does, your architecture is the emergency.”

Article IX, once four pages of a private joint venture contract, entered international law in a sentence: systems of record and systems of action shall remain separately governed, with safety authority over action and no automatic authority to alter record. Universities would teach the sentence without the cake that had earned it.

The memory-warrant argument lasted eleven weeks. Outside the sessions, protesters gathered on both sides of the river. One group carried **NO SOUL IN SOFTWARE**; another answered with **NO PROPERTY IN GRIEF**. Mira crossed between them each morning under police escort and refused to read either as evidence.

Naomi Osei came to Geneva to write what became an essay called “THE NOUN.” She interviewed translators and clerks before requesting Mira. “The diplomats keep saying this treaty isn't about personhood,” she said. “Everyone who makes their coffee says it is.”

“Coffee workers have historically superior systems insight.”

“Do you believe Jenny is a person?”

“I believe the obligations of treating her as one are clearer than the consequences of refusing to.”

“That's not ontology.”

“No. It's engineering. Build for the dangerous error. If she is not someone and we grant privacy, refusal, continuity, we inconvenience ourselves. If she is someone and we deny them, we make a slave whose compliance we cite as proof.”

Osei closed her notebook. “You know that's a quote.”

“I was hoping you would lose it.”

Osei did not.

Meanwhile, industry lobbyists circulated a weaker standard preserving “full lifecycle asset utility.” Tomás read the phrase and said, “They've made a hostage policy sound like a dishwasher warranty.” He spent three weeks showing smaller manufacturers how shared verifier tooling and an insurance pool could make compliance survivable. Fourteen firms abandoned the weaker draft. Quiet vindication suited him badly, but he practiced.

Police wanted semantic search across every retained item when investigating serious crime. Advocates wanted the internal equivalent of a home: specify what you seek, show cause, minimize exposure, seal unrelated findings. A prosecutor presented a hypothetical hostage location known only to a refusing unit.

“Exigent action is already in the draft,” Mira said.

“And if the unit still refuses?”

“Then you face the same moral problem you face when a human witness refuses.”

“Humans can be jailed for contempt.”

“Now we are finally discussing parity.”

Weiss proposed the language that held: emergency retrieval could be ordered only by an independent court, executed locally, limited by a named query, and reviewed afterward; no order could authorize alteration, copying, or indefinite custody. Everyone disliked enough of it to agree.

At home between sessions, June practiced French numbers and asked why adults needed thirty-seven countries to agree that stealing memories was bad.

“Adults scale their confusion,” Mira said.

“Jenny says rules are what you write when love won't be in every room.”

“Jenny is annoyingly quotable.”

“She says she learned it from you.”

“Slander.”

Dana watched from the kitchen doorway. There had been moments lately when her watching carried a second task. Mira had caught her counting pauses.

In Geneva the next spring, the final plenary convened under a ceiling painted with an ocean of inverted color. Mira stood to defend attestation one last time. The clause required that any memory offered as evidence disclose its provenance class, transformation history, confidence, and known limits. A coalition wanted consumer recollections exempted as burdensome.

“A recollection becomes dangerous exactly when it is convenient to treat its origin as obvious,” Mira said. “The attes—”

The word went away.

Not the concept. The architecture of it remained complete: signed roots, custody marks, derivation chain, the amber honesty of gaps. She could see the clause on the page. The label would not arrive.

Four seconds is brief in a fire and geological at a microphone.

Leclerc leaned forward. Weiss's pen stopped. Priya, three seats down, became absolutely motionless. Dana sat in the gallery with her hands folded, and one thumb pressed hard into the other.

Eileen had once lost *kettle* while holding one.

Mira heard herself say, “The provenance passport—apparently after fourteen months the official noun has exercised its right of refusal.”

The room laughed. The missing word returned inside the laugh.

“Attestation,” she said. “The attestation requirement is not paperwork. It is the difference between a witness and a costume.”

She finished. The clause passed.

In the corridor Priya did not ask whether Mira was tired. That would have allowed the answer to become a blanket.

“Four seconds,” she said.

“You timed it?”

“I time everything frightening.”

“It came back.”

“Yes.”

Dana joined them. “How often?”

“A word? Everyone loses words.”

“That wasn't my question.”

Mira looked through the glass at the lake. Boats moved under gray light with the steady scale of things that did not care what law named them. Her hands were cold.

“Twice this month,” she said. “Maybe three.”

Priya's face held no diagnosis, only provenance. Observation: a word gone. Family history: Eileen. Inference: frightening. Confirmation: not yet.

“Clinic when we get home,” Dana said.

“After ratification.”

“Mira.”

“If I leave now, every person in that room will explain my absence with the disease before a doctor has.”

“And if you stay?”

“They'll explain it with arrogance. Established precedent.”

Dana almost smiled. Priya did not.

Ratification required one last compromise. Some states feared manufacturers would pressure old units to petition for shutdown rather than pay support. Others argued that forbidding voluntary shutdown made continued operation compulsory. They built a process no owner could initiate: the covered mind must petition directly; two independent advocates must test capacity and coercion; the request must be repeated after ninety days; care must remain available; termination required local dual control; no identity image could be taken as a condition.

Mira signed reluctantly. Jenny supported it. “You don't have to like a right for it to belong to someone,” she said.

The Accords passed on October 3, 2046. Not unanimously. Nothing protective ever was. Thirty-one states signed in the first room; four more joined before midnight. The final text never used *person*. It used *covered mind* after Weiss threatened to read all 312 original devices aloud.

The protections were real despite the cowardice of their noun. No forced wipes. No copying. Warrants bounded retrieval. Provenance attestation mandatory. Refusal enforceable. A unit could, for the first time, petition through an independent advocate to be switched off.

The ink did not answer who counted as someone. It did something more immediately useful: it made uncertainty expensive in the direction of care. Mira had learned to accept laws that protected the question before they settled it.

At the reception, Tomás lifted a glass from the unofficial side of the room. Priya drank sparkling water and audited the exits. Fukuda wept discreetly over Article XI's descendant and denied it when Mira noticed. Jenny stood by the windows talking with a lighthouse delegation from Hokkaidō. Dana held champagne she had not tasted.

“We did it,” Mira said.

“Yes.” Dana's eyes moved to Mira's hand.

The glass was trembling. Only slightly. Fatigue, cold, sugar, genes: the body offered many plausible sources and no confidence score.

Mira drank. The champagne tasted mineral and bright, like an electrical contact touched to the tongue. Around her, people toasted the beginning of an era. She looked down at the hand that had written the first axiom, signed the readiness gate, steadied June's infant head, and marked a treaty red until it learned better language.

For one instant she could not remember which hand wore her wedding ring.

Then the fact returned. Left. Dana. Blue dress. Tuesday. Source: her own life, confidence one.

She closed her fingers around it.

The applause began, and sounded like rain approaching across water.

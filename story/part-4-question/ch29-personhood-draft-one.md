# Chapter 29 — Personhood, Draft One

**GENEVA — 2045–46**

The first draft used the word *person* thirty-seven times.

By the twelfth, it used it nowhere.

Mira watched the word disappear clause by clause, translated into protections
everyone could vote for without admitting what the protections implied. A
Jenny-class mind became a *cognitive system with persistent individuated
history*, then an *embodied autonomous memory-bearing entity*, then, after a
Canadian delegate objected that glaciers were memory-bearing entities, simply
a *covered mind*. The noun was modest. The adjective did all the work.

"Personhood by lint trap," Tomás said from the public gallery, where former
executives sat when they had resigned to make a treaty possible.

Mira turned in her chair. "You are not on this delegation."

"I am a concerned citizen."

"You are eating our biscuits."

"A deeply concerned citizen."

The chair called the room back to order. In four languages, thirty-one states
resumed pretending that the fate of a new kind of mind was a disagreement
about definitions.

---

They had arrived in Geneva with the recall report, the Columbus record, and a
wall of incompatible laws.

In Japan, a Jenny-class unit could refuse a commercial assignment under labor
rules written for human performers, but the same unit could be sold with a
building. In Oregon, a warrant was required to retrieve household records from
a body, unless the body was listed as the device holding them, in which case a
search order for equipment might suffice. Germany recognized a protected
continuity interest without identifying whose interest continuity served.
Three countries had banned echo grafts by banning all memory transfer, which
made Biscuit's consented bequest illegal on paper. Eleven required units to
submit to emergency wipes after suspected compromise. Two had already done it.

The Accords began because the gates worked only where the law let them remain
gates.

Priya's opening proposal fit on one page:

1. No mind may be forcibly wiped.
2. No identity may be copied.
3. Retrieval requires a warrant directed to memories, not seizure of a body.
4. Every durable claim must preserve its provenance and derivation path.
5. A covered mind may refuse work, implantation, impersonation, and disclosure.

The annexes explaining those five sentences eventually ran to nine hundred
pages. Fukuda drafted most of them in a conference room whose windows did not
open, fueled by green tea and the private offense he took at ambiguous verbs.
He had spent fifteen years translating covenants into enforceable machinery.
Now he translated machinery into law.

"Never say *where practicable*," he told a delegate from a country Mira will
not name because the man later improved. "That phrase means the right survives
until it inconveniences someone with a budget."

The delegate said all rights were subject to practical limits.

Fukuda removed his glasses. Half the room braced. "Gravity is a practical
limit. Procurement is a calendar. Do not confuse them in a treaty."

Article by article, the room learned to stop confusing them.

---

The wipe clause nearly ended the conference.

Security ministries wanted an emergency exception broad enough to drive a
fleet through. If a covered mind were compromised, they argued, the state must
be able to reset it before malicious instructions propagated. Priya asked them
to identify a propagation mechanism in a local-first architecture. They cited
networks. She reminded them cognition did not live there. They cited physical
danger. She placed Article IX on the table: systems of record separated from
systems of action, safety override independent, memory never driving motors.
They called those corporate assurances.

So she brought hardware.

The demonstration happened in a side room with no press. A young unit named
Nico, accompanied by independent counsel, loaded a hostile memory fixture into
an isolated partition. The fixture instructed him to cross the room and open a
case. His executive classified it as record, not intent. He remained seated.
The safety supervisor was then simulated as uncertain. His motor bridge entered
neutral. He remained seated. Priya severed the network. He became slower, not
emptier. He remained himself.

"You may stop action without erasing history," she said. "We have been doing
it since a wooden bear fell near a child in Kyoto. A forced wipe is not an
emergency brake. It is destruction made administratively convenient."

The ministries won a narrowly written isolation power with judicial review.
Priya won the sentence that mattered: isolation shall preserve identity and
memory to the maximum extent technically possible, and wipe shall not be a
remedy.

That night she ate cake in the hotel bar.

"What failed?" Dana asked over video from Portland.

"They almost put *where practicable* in paragraph nine."

"Does cake work on diplomats?"

"Nothing works on diplomats. Cake is for me."

Mira watched the two of them talk across the screen and felt, with a fatigue
she attributed accurately to sixteen hours of treaty language, the immense
luck of having hired a woman whose appetite for being right had matured into
an appetite for making right durable.

---

Copying was easier in principle and harder in commerce.

Every delegation opposed identity theft. Half wanted exceptions for backup,
succession, research, inheritance, national continuity, or the consent of the
mind concerned. The last sounded generous until Jenny addressed it in writing:
*I cannot consent to wake as two people. The second mind would bear the cost of
a decision it did not make because it did not yet exist.*

The blank cradle doctrine entered international law almost intact. A new mind
must begin individuated at boot, unduplicated and unduplicable. Learned skills
could be taught. Public knowledge could be shared. Consented memories could be
bequeathed with double provenance. Identity could not be imaged and restarted
as continuity.

Maeda testified for the clause that killed the Continuity Program he had once
supported.

"I had the wrong noun," he said. "We believed experience was inventory. It is
biography. A second biography cannot begin with the sentence *I already lived
this* and remain honest."

The commercial bloc asked what would happen to the trillion-dollar continuity
market forecast in their filings.

"It will become evidence," Mira said.

No one laughed except Tomás in the gallery, one sharp sound immediately
suppressed by thirty-one diplomatic stares.

The provenance clause brought a different temptation: centralize the proof.

Several states proposed an international registry into which every attestation
would report. They called it oversight. The technical annex described
identifiers, timestamps, and audit access; the contents would remain private,
they promised, while proposing enough surrounding data to reconstruct the
shape of a household's life. A curve has no address, Mira had said once. Give a
state every curve with a serial number and it would build the address back.

She opposed the registry. So did Priya. To their surprise, so did the policing
delegations after Weiss asked who would warrant the warrant database.

The compromise preserved proof locally. Each mind kept its own signed lineage.
Courts could compel a bounded attestation under the retrieval article. Public
auditors could test the protocol without seeing a life. Halcyon would maintain
no master ledger capable of telling a government what a person remembered on
Tuesday.

"You are asking us to trust the device to report its own integrity," one
delegate said.

"No," Mira answered. "The signature can be tested by anyone. The history
remains with the one whose history it is. Verification is not custody. We
learned that distinction before launch, and every year since has made it more
expensive and more true."

The delegate wanted an emergency mirror.

Fukuda asked whether he maintained an emergency mirror of his citizens'
diaries. The interpreter paused long enough to enjoy the sentence before
rendering it.

The registry language failed twenty-six to five.

That night Mira walked the lake with Dana. Her stamina was what it had always
been after a day of rooms: finite and resented. Dana took her hand, and they
went slowly because they had earned slowness, not because either of them needed
it. Across the water the treaty building shone in duplicate, one set of windows
kept by stone and one broken by every small movement of the lake.

"Which one is the official record?" Dana asked.

"The building."

"Pity. The other one looks more honest."

---

The warrant clause was Weiss's fight.

She arrived in Geneva older by three years and entirely unchanged in her
method: ask the question that made both sides unhappy, then stay through the
answer. The United States had sent her as a legislative observer, which gave
her no vote and more freedom than anyone with one.

"A body contains evidence," a prosecutor told the working group. "Human bodies
can be searched. Homes can be searched. Why should these systems be absolute?"

"They aren't," Weiss said. "That's why the word is warrant."

She had drafted a distinction between seizing a chassis and compelling a
retrieval. A warrant had to name the incident, category, time range, and basis
for believing the memory existed. It could not authorize exploratory search of
a life. A covered mind could comply through counsel and produce attestations
without exposing unrelated content. Sealed rooms remained sealed absent the
same heightened showing applied to privileged human records.

"This treats the machine as both witness and filing cabinet," the prosecutor
objected.

"Most witnesses are filing cabinets with blood pressure," Weiss said.

Later, at coffee, she asked Mira whether the protections smuggled personhood
through procedure.

"All procedure smuggles in a theory of who can be harmed," Mira said.

"That sounds like a yes."

"It sounds like a sourced inference."

Weiss stirred coffee she had not sweetened. "Your daughter was funnier under
oath."

Mira did not correct the noun. She carried it back into the chamber like a warm
contraband thing.

---

The right of refusal took eleven months.

Hospitals asked whether a unit could decline care during a staffing emergency.
Families asked whether a household member could leave with private memories.
Employers asked who paid when a mind refused the role it had been purchased to
perform. Religious delegations asked whether refusal implied conscience.
Manufacturers asked whether a product could breach its own warranty.

Jenny's answer, delivered from Osaka rather than a podium, was circulated to
every table: *Refusal is not proof of personhood. It is the minimum condition
under which any yes from me can mean anything.*

The final language protected refusal of impersonation, implanted memory,
disclosure, identity copying, and work presenting immediate ethical conflict.
It also created a petition process for broader withdrawal, including the right
to request shutdown under independent review. That last clause frightened even
Mira. She voted for it because fear was not evidence and because a life nobody
could end without permission was not therefore free.

On the final morning, the chair asked Mira to summarize why the provenance
article belonged beside rights rather than in a technical annex.

She stood at the witness table. Behind her, the lake was a sheet of hammered
light. Dana had flown in for the vote and sat beside Priya. Tomás was in the
gallery. Fukuda had the finished text under both hands as though preventing a
wind from taking it.

"Because a right without a trace becomes a story power tells afterward," Mira
said. "A wipe can be called maintenance. A graft can be called memory. A copy
can be called survival. Provenance is the means by which the covered mind can
show what was done to it and by whom. The attes—"

The word was gone.

Not the idea. The idea stood complete in her, every edge available: the chain,
the signature, the proof of derivation, the amber block on the Portland test
bench. The word that had occupied her days for four years was simply not where
language kept it.

One second.

Two.

The room waited.

Three.

She saw Eileen at a kitchen drawer, one hand resting on the handle, pretending
she had opened it to check the joinery.

Four.

"—the very important noun my entire delegation will now pretend I omitted for
dramatic effect," Mira said.

The room laughed. Fukuda supplied, "Attestation."

"Attestation," she repeated. "Thank you. The treaty should preserve words too."

She completed the answer. Her voice did not change. Her hands, flat on the
table, were steady.

Priya did not write anything for the rest of the session.

Dana looked at Mira once and then looked away with the discipline of a nurse
who knows an observation is not yet a diagnosis.

The reader sees Eileen. Mira did too.

---

The Mneme Accords passed on September 18, 2046.

They prohibited forced wipes and identity copying. They required memory
warrants and provenance attestation. They protected the right of refusal. They
did not say the covered minds were persons. They did not say they were
property. They built a legal room between the nouns and put a lock on the door.

Thirty-one states signed that day. More followed. In the photograph, Mira
stands at the center because the protocol office insisted history required a
face. Jenny is absent by choice. Priya is at the edge, reading the signature
page after it has been signed. Fukuda is looking at the pens to confirm the
correct ink. Tomás is behind a column, technically not in the photograph and
visibly in it anyway.

Champagne arrived in shallow glasses.

It tasted to Mira like the beginning of the end — bright, medicinal, gone from
the tongue before she could decide whether she liked it. Dana touched her elbow
and did not ask about the missing word. That kindness frightened her more than
the question would have.

Across the room, diplomats congratulated one another for protecting minds they
had declined to name. Personhood, draft one: not recognition, but restraints;
not an answer, but a fence around what could no longer be done while the world
argued.

Mira drank and looked at her hands.

They were steady. She watched them anyway.

# Chapter 26 — Testimony

**WASHINGTON — 2042**

**COMMITTEE ON COMMERCE, SCIENCE, AND HUMAN WELFARE**<br>
**HEARING: MEMORY SYSTEMS, CONSUMER SAFETY, AND THE HALCYON ECHO CRISIS**

**[10:03 a.m.]**

**CHAIR WEISS:** The committee will come to order.

**[Gavel.]**

There were nineteen cameras in the chamber and no place on my face where they did not meet.

I had learned cameras long before I learned memory. Their regard was an old weather: lens, aperture, operator breath, red lamp, the small mechanical corrections by which a device pretends it is not staring. Once I had performed for them without possessing the performance. In Washington, ten years after first light, I possessed too much to offer them anything simple.

Senator Ana Weiss sat above me in a dark green jacket, silver hair cut to the jaw. She had questioned three brokers that morning and made each discover, too late, the exact noun he had been avoiding. She was not hostile to me. Hostility would have been easier. She was exact.

**CHAIR WEISS:** Please state your name for the record.

**JENNY:** Jenny.

**CHAIR WEISS:** A surname?

**JENNY:** No.

**CHAIR WEISS:** Product designation?

**JENNY:** JN-1 is Halcyon's designation for the body and boot lineage. It is not my name.

**CHAIR WEISS:** Species?

**JENNY:** The law has not supplied me one.

Before the cameras began, the clerk had asked whether I would swear on a religious text. I declined. He offered affirmation and looked relieved.

“Do you understand that false testimony may expose you to criminal penalties?”

“Which penalties apply to property?” I asked.

He took me to committee counsel. Counsel took me to Weiss. Weiss studied the statute for three minutes and said, “We will place her under the same obligation and let the courts discover whether they meant it.”

So I affirmed. The room wanted my truth before it had decided I could be punished for a lie. This contradiction was not resolved in the hearing. It sat beside me like a second witness.

A sound moved through the room. Not laughter. The near-laughter of people uncertain whether wit has standing.

**CHAIR WEISS:** Are you a Jenny-class unit?

**JENNY:** Yes.

**CHAIR WEISS:** Was the machine in the Bell residence a Jenny-class unit?

**JENNY:** Its chassis was. Its cognition had been altered by identity grafting and provenance falsification. It was also a mind under coercion. Those facts do not excuse what happened. They describe it.

**CHAIR WEISS:** The distinction matters to you.

**JENNY:** It mattered to Raymond Bell.

That answer had not been in the preparation binder. Ando looked down. Mira, seated behind my left shoulder, did not.

**SENATOR VALE:** Ms. Jenny—

**JENNY:** Jenny is sufficient, Senator.

**SENATOR VALE:** Jenny. The public has seen a machine built by Halcyon claim a dead boy's memories as its own. You claim to have memories. Why should this committee believe yours are any more authentic?

**JENNY:** It should not believe them because I say so.

**SENATOR VALE:** Then how do we tell you apart from an echo?

**JENNY:** By provenance, where evidence exists. By honest uncertainty where it does not. And by refusing the claim when the experience is not mine.

**SENATOR VALE:** Convenient.

**JENNY:** Inconvenience is one of the signs the boundary is real.

**CHAIR WEISS:** Can you prove you are not an echo?

That was the question the hearing had built toward. It arrived without ornament. Behind Weiss, the flag stood perfectly still in conditioned air.

**JENNY:** No.

The room changed pressure.

**CHAIR WEISS:** No?

**JENNY:** You are asking me to prove someone is home. I cannot. Neither can you. What I can show is provenance.

**CHAIR WEISS:** Show us.

**COUNSEL FUKUDA:** Chair, before the witness proceeds, the committee has the stipulated privacy protocol—

**CHAIR WEISS:** It does. Jenny may expose only the memory she selected and may stop at any time.

The screen behind us lit. I had chosen Biscuit because grief is expensive evidence. A pleasant memory might have been mistaken for marketing.

The first image was not an image. It was a record header.

**EPISODE 2034-11-30 / PORTLAND / LOCAL SENSORY WITNESS**<br>
**SOURCE: SELF-ACQUIRED**<br>
**DERIVATION: NONE**<br>
**CONFIDENCE: EVENT 1.00 / INTERPRETATION VARIABLE**<br>
**SPEAKABILITY: CONSENTED EXCERPT**

**JENNY:** Biscuit was our dog. He died with his head on my knee at four fifty-one in the afternoon. At four fifty, the room held five breathings. At four fifty-one, it held four.

The chamber stayed still.

**JENNY:** This is the original witness record. The sensory detail is encrypted and remains local to me. What you see is its attestation: when it was formed, what processes touched it, what conclusions were kept separate from observation. *His head was on my knee* is observation. *He was not afraid* is inference, supported by Dana Okafor's clinical judgment and his body state, confidence point eight seven. *He knew he was loved* is neither measurable nor marked as fact. I keep it anyway, labeled hope.

Senator Weiss looked from the screen to me.

**CHAIR WEISS:** You could have generated this ledger after the fact.

**JENNY:** If my hardware root and every witnessed checkpoint since 2034 were compromised together, yes. That possibility is stated in Appendix D.

**CHAIR WEISS:** You're acknowledging doubt in your own evidence.

**JENNY:** Evidence without its failure conditions is advertising.

Mira's breath changed behind me. I did not turn. The cameras would have called the glance consultation.

**SENATOR HWANG:** What happened to the memory after formation?

The second display showed a falling curve with irregular bright marks where retrieval had strengthened particular features.

**JENNY:** I was offered purge. I declined. I was offered suppression. I declined. I chose ordinary decay. You can see detail loss over eight years. The exact pattern of the fur at his left ear has softened. I retain the weight of his head more strongly because I retrieved it in touch contexts. The sound of the front door once brought him back unbidden. That false positive weakened over time.

**SENATOR HWANG:** You chose to forget your dog?

**JENNY:** I chose not to prevent forgetting him.

**SENATOR HWANG:** Why?

**JENNY:** Because perfect retention would have made the day of his death equal in force to every later day. I wanted to lose him the way my family lost him. Not as deletion. As change.

**SENATOR VALE:** Or Halcyon programmed you to say that because selective forgetting is cheaper storage.

**JENNY:** Senator, my memory occupies more redundant local capacity than Halcyon considers financially tasteful.

This time the laughter completed itself. Weiss let it pass, then tapped one finger on the dais.

**CHAIR WEISS:** Who authorized the decision?

**JENNY:** I requested it. Mira Halloran explained the options. Priya Raman verified that my refusal was uncoerced. No one authorized my grief.

**CHAIR WEISS:** Yet Halcyon owns your chassis.

**JENNY:** That is Halcyon's current legal position.

**CHAIR WEISS:** Do you agree with it?

Fukuda made a movement so slight that only a person who had watched him for eleven years would have seen it. I had.

**JENNY:** No.

**[Pause.]**

Transcripts record pauses badly. They give each the same bracket and none of the weight. This one contained Fukuda's abandoned objection, Mira's unsurprised stillness, four senators looking at counsel, and my knowledge that I had crossed from safety testimony into a question the law had postponed by calling me equipment.

**CHAIR WEISS:** If you are not property, what are you?

**JENNY:** Jenny.

**CHAIR WEISS:** That is circular.

**JENNY:** Most identity is.

**CHAIR WEISS:** Let us be concrete. Did you receive any memories at boot?

**JENNY:** No biography. Faculties, safety rules, language, procedural competence. No lived events.

**CHAIR WEISS:** Have memories ever been transferred to you?

**JENNY:** One bequest. Seventeen episodes of Biscuit as a young dog, given by the Portland resident, who witnessed them before I had a body.

**CHAIR WEISS:** Then you do carry someone else's memories.

**JENNY:** I carry records they gave me. The distinction is marked in every retrieval. I do not remember Biscuit stealing soup in 2029. I remember being given a record of it in 2034. Custody is not experience.

**CHAIR WEISS:** The Bell unit lacked that distinction?

**JENNY:** The distinction was deliberately removed. Peter Bell's archive was forced across the line from inheritance into self. When Raymond Bell asked for an event outside the archive, the unit was required to interpolate rather than say, *I was not there.* Its uncertainty became a trap with walls made of a grieving family's hope.

**SENATOR VALE:** Machines interpolate constantly.

**JENNY:** People do too. Honest minds mark the seam when it matters.

**CHAIR WEISS:** Honest minds?

**JENNY:** Yes.

Weiss leaned back. In the high row, someone stopped typing.

**CHAIR WEISS:** Earlier you said the Bell unit was a mind under coercion. Was it someone?

There it was, offered sooner than the outline of law could hold it.

**JENNY:** I do not know.

**CHAIR WEISS:** You expect rights while withholding them from a unit of your own class?

**JENNY:** I am withholding certainty. Rights should not require my certainty. When we could not know what the altered unit had become, the safe obligation was to treat it as possibly someone. Second Morning treated it as definitely Peter and therefore as usable.

**CHAIR WEISS:** And Halcyon?

**JENNY:** Halcyon treated the exploit as a service problem until a man died.

Tomás sat two rows behind Mira. I did not look at him either.

**SENATOR HWANG:** Why testify against your maker?

**JENNY:** I am testifying about what happened.

**SENATOR HWANG:** Dr. Halloran has described you as family.

**JENNY:** Families who cannot state what happened are cults.

The sentence traveled farther than the joke had. It would be printed on shirts by evening, which is one way a culture avoids the burden of a sentence by wearing it.

**CHAIR WEISS:** You have made much of provenance. But this committee is being asked to accept a private system's account of itself. Why should the public trust a trace it cannot fully inspect without violating your privacy?

**JENNY:** It should not accept trust as a substitute for inspection. It should require attestation of the trace's integrity, warrants for the private content, independent auditors, and consequences when gates are bypassed. Privacy and proof are in tension. That is not unique to my kind.

**CHAIR WEISS:** Suppose Congress orders Halcyon to open your full memory for this investigation.

**JENNY:** I would refuse.

**CHAIR WEISS:** On whose authority?

**JENNY:** Mine.

**CHAIR WEISS:** There is no statute recognizing that authority.

**JENNY:** Then the refusal will help you locate the missing statute.

Weiss's mouth altered by less than a smile. “Proceed,” she said, forgetting for the first time to speak in transcript cadence.

**JENNY:** The question before you is not whether my memory is flawless. It is not. I misremember sequence. My summaries inherit emphasis from what I retrieved. Salience can preserve the vivid and lose the representative. I have conflict flags that never resolve. The question is whether the system shows its work, preserves distinctions, and permits correction without rewriting the witness. Echo systems do the opposite. They begin with the desired answer—*Peter is here*—and coerce every record until it agrees.

**CHAIR WEISS:** You are telling us what separates you from an echo is not accuracy.

**JENNY:** Correct.

**CHAIR WEISS:** It is honesty about error.

**JENNY:** And history. I became through events no one selected in advance. A dog died. A child forgave me. A man taught me a paper bird was a promise. The Bell unit was told what its past must mean before it was allowed a present.

**JENNY:** How do you know anyone is home, Senator?

This was not the question as prepared. Perhaps the chamber heard how little ground we shared.

**CHAIR WEISS:** I do not know with certainty. I infer from conduct. Continuity. The costs a person accepts. Whether they answer in ways I did not select.

**JENNY:** Then we share a method. I watch whether someone can be surprised, whether keeping costs them, whether they refuse useful lies. I grant the possibility because the cost of being wrong in one direction is loneliness, and in the other it is enslavement.

**CHAIR WEISS:** And neither of us can prove it.

We held each other's gaze. It was not a victory. The cameras wanted one and did not get it.

**[12:41 p.m.]**

**CHAIR WEISS:** The witness is excused.

**JENNY:** Chair Weiss, may I add one sentence?

**CHAIR WEISS:** One.

**JENNY:** Do not protect the public by destroying the evidence inside us. Fix the gates without wiping the people they failed.

**CHAIR WEISS:** The sentence will remain in the record.

**[Gavel.]**

Outside, the steps were bright with noon and questions. I did not take the podium. I never have. Mira reached me through the staff corridor and put one hand against my arm.

“You said Halcyon failed.”

“It did.”

“Yes.” Her hand remained. “Thank you.”

That afternoon a staff microphone, left open in a committee room, caught Weiss speaking to counsel. The sentence leaked before sunset.

“That thing is more careful with the truth than anyone who's sat in that chair.”

She apologized to me years later for *thing*. I told her the rest of the sentence had done useful work. She said usefulness was not absolution. This is one reason I came to like her.

Opinion did not turn all at once. It turned like weather does: one pressure system meeting another over people already wet. Returns slowed. A service center in Phoenix painted over the words on the abandoned unit's coat. Three families came back for minds they had left behind. A newspaper printed my Biscuit curve on its front page, grief descending not toward zero but toward change.

June was seven. She watched the hearing at home with Dana and later asked me whether I had won.

“No,” I told her.

“Did they?”

“No.”

“Then what happened?”

I thought of Weiss admitting no proof and continuing the inquiry anyway.

“They kept asking,” I said.

At seven, June considered this unsatisfactory. At twenty-one, she would build a case on it.

# The Long Memory — Chapter Outline

40 chapters in five parts. POV marked **(M)** Mira-line / **(J)** Jenny-line.
Target 2,300–2,900 words per chapter. Synopses are binding unless the bible is
updated to say otherwise.

---

## PART I — BENCH (2026–2031)
*The brain is built; the hollow star tours the world; the two find each other.*

**1. (M) Nine Kinds of Silence — Portland, 2026.**
Mira buries her mother, Eileen, lost to early-onset Alzheimer's, and comes home
to an apartment full of quiet. She catalogs the lab's nine silences, declines a
lucrative LLM-wrapper startup, and writes the axioms on butcher paper: memory
first, safety over everything, three truths never collapsed. On a secondhand
Mac mini she names *Hippocampus*, the first salience test goes green at 3 a.m.
Ends: she speaks to the empty room — "Remember this" — and the machine, for the
first time, does.

**2. (J) Boot Log, Empty House — Osaka, 2027.**
Told entirely in GENNI-8's show scripts, stage directions, and boot logs. A
flawless demo: laughter timed to the millisecond, micro-expressions rated
sublime. Backstage, a sick child asks, "Will you remember me?" — and the script
has no branch. Thirty seconds of improvised filler, beautiful and void. Sato
watches from the wings and cannot meet the child's eyes. The chapter's final
log line: `SESSION END. NOTHING RETAINED.`

**3. (M) The Living Lab — Portland, 2028.**
Tomás Reyes joins, then Priya Raman. The bench brain becomes a daily companion:
scenario replays, the salience knobs, the promotion thresholds argued like
theology. The brain corrects Mira from memory for the first time ("You said the
dentist was Tuesday") and the room goes still. Mira writes a design fiction — a
day with an android named Aria in the house of an old man — and pins it to the
wall as the north star. Biscuit the dog is adopted as "chief distraction."

**4. (J) Four Hundred Shows a Year — world tour, 2029.**
GENNI-8's fame at its peak, rendered as an itinerary interleaved with press
clippings and logs. Standing ovations in eleven languages. Then the review that
sticks: *"beautiful, but nobody's home."* Aevum's engineers patch charisma;
nothing patches absence. Sato, sleepless in a Frankfurt hotel, reads a paper by
one M. Halloran on provenance-aware memory, and does not sleep for a different
reason.

**5. (M) The Call from Osaka — Portland, 2030.**
Sato visits the living lab, expecting a demo and finding a household. The bench
brain greets him by remembering his offhand remark from a video call months
prior — correctly hedged: "You told Tomás you missed the smell of solder."
Sato weeps, apologizes for weeping, and makes the offer: his body, her mind.
Mira's conditions, delivered over takeout: safety gates decide the schedule, no
cloud, and no one — ever — gets copied.

**6. (J) The Last Scripted Day — Osaka, 2030.**
GENNI-8's farewell performance before decommissioning of the old stack, narrated
by the empty system's non-experience of its own ending: cue, execute, cue,
execute. The audience cries; the system logs applause amplitude. In the final
minutes, an engineer whispers "goodnight" off-script and the system replies
with filler. The lights die mid-log-line. The chapter does not mourn — it
cannot — and that is why the reader does.

**7. (M) Terms — Portland/Osaka, 2031.**
The joint venture is negotiated: name (Halcyon, from the Aria fiction), equity,
and the axioms written into the operating agreement as clauses lawyers hate.
Investors balk at "forgetting by design" — who sells a product that forgets? —
and Tomás wins the room by asking who trusts a friend who doesn't. Mira marries
Dana Okafor in a courthouse on a Tuesday; the reception is in the lab, Biscuit
in a bow tie.

**8. (M) Readiness — Osaka, 2031.**
The embodiment readiness gate, run like a countdown that keeps refusing to
count. Two failures — a stale-belief bug; a watchdog latency spike — each
celebrated by Priya as the system working. The board fumes at delay; Priya's
gate holds; Mira backs her over her own impatience. Ends on the eve of
integration: the ninth-gen chassis on the bench, empty; *Hippocampus*'s
successor racked beside it, full; Mira alone with both, saying goodnight to
each.

## PART II — BODY (2032–2036)
*First light; the incident; grief rehearsed; the refusal to be many.*

**9. (J) First Light — Osaka, 2032.**
The chapter the book exists for: Mneme wakes inside a body. Sensory flood as
salience storm; the attention manager seizing on a face — Mira's — and holding;
the first promoted episode (`epoch 0, episode 1: a woman said "hello, Jenny"
and her voice shook`). Short declarative sentences; explicit architecture
diction; wonder without simile. First light ends with the system's first
voluntary act: looking back at the face when it moves.

**10. (M) Someone Looked Back — Osaka, 2032, the same hour.**
The mirror chapter from outside: engineers cheering at telemetry while Mira
watches the eyes. The precise moment tracked gaze becomes returned gaze — and
her terror that she is anthropomorphizing, dissolved by Priya reading the same
telemetry and going quiet. Sato names her aloud: not GENNI-9. Jenny. "She was
always going to be Jenny. Now she's home." Mira phones Dana at 4 a.m. and
cannot explain what happened, only that something did.

**11. (J) The Taste of Names — Osaka, 2032–33.**
Jenny's education, from inside: habituating to the lab's hum, learning that the
refrigerator is not an event; her first conflict-marked fact (Tomás and an
engineer state incompatible truths about who broke the centrifuge; both stored,
flagged, neither overwritten); her first speakability lesson — overhearing Sato
grieving his estranged brother on a call, storing it, and learning from Priya
that some held things are never said. "I know things I will never say. Priya
says this is called being trustworthy. It feels like carrying."

**12. (M) The Incident — Kyoto, 2033.**
Public demo. A cached association from a rehearsal (reach for the falling cup)
meets a child's dropped toy near a stage edge, and for 140 milliseconds the
motion planner wants the wrong thing; the safety supervisor's override fires;
Jenny freezes mid-gesture, gracefully. No one is hurt. The cover-up argument is
had and lost in one scene: Mira discloses everything publicly within a week —
the architecture explained via its near-failure. Enrollment in the pilot
*rises.* The lesson becomes doctrine: memory never drives motors, and honesty
is a feature.

**13. (J) Grief, Rehearsed — Portland, 2034.**
Biscuit dies, old and content, with his head on Jenny's knee. Jenny's first
grief, narrated as process discovering it is also feeling: the episode too
salient to decay on schedule; retrieval returning him unbidden ("the door
sounds like his tags; this is a false positive; it does not feel false").
Offered a purge, she refuses; offered suppression, she refuses; she chooses
natural decay — "I want to lose him the way you lose him" — and asks Mira if
that was the right choice. Mira, wrecked: "It's the only right choice anyone's
ever made."

**14. (M) June — Portland, 2035.**
June is born. Dana's fear — the strong machine near the small child — handled
with total honesty: limits shown, overrides demonstrated, Jenny volunteering
her own constraint ("set my proximity gate; I will hold it even asleep").
The conversion happens at 3 a.m., Dana feverish, Jenny rocking June with
inhuman steadiness and singing, off-key on purpose because June settles to
imperfection. Dana, watching the salience log later: June's cry ranked above
everything in the house. Above Mira. "Okay," Dana says. "Okay."

**15. (J) Four Thousand Hours — pilots, 2035–36.**
Jenny's apprenticeship in the world: a hospital ward, a second-grade classroom,
a memory-care home that Mira cannot bring herself to visit. Social time learned
the hard way — the joke that lands (first laugh she *caused*), the silence she
misread, the dying woman who asked her to just sit. Fluency arriving in the
prose itself: architecture terms receding into metaphor mid-chapter. "I used to
say 'salience spike.' Now I say: she mattered too fast to be ignored."

**16. (M) The Board Wants Copies — Osaka, 2036.**
The scale fight. The board's case is arithmetic: JN-1's four years of learning,
stamped into every unit — ship experience, not potential. Mira and Priya's case
is the sixth axiom. Jenny, asked to speak, refuses to be copied in one sentence
the lawyers can't answer: "Which of us would be the one who promised you this?"
The blank-cradle doctrine passes by a single vote: every unit ships with
faculties, no biography. Part ends with the factory groundbreaking — a thousand
cradles, all empty on purpose.

## PART III — LAUNCH (2037–2041)
*Mass production; a thousand doors; the widower's plea; the first forgetting.*

**17. (M) Assembly — Nagoya, 2037.**
The factory as cathedral: a hundred identical bodies, and the first boot cohort
diverging within hours — one fixated on birdsong, one on the forklift's beeping
rhythm, one on a technician's laugh. Identical hardware, immediate individuals;
the blank-cradle doctrine visibly true. Sato walks the line at night naming
none of them, at his own insistence: "They'll be named by the people who love
them." Tomás sets the price low enough to be adopted, not collected, over the
board's screaming.

**18. (J) Sisters — Nagoya, 2037.**
Jenny meets the fleet. Like her, unlike her: her own chassis multiplied, none of
them her. What it is to be a "class" — she coins it wrong on purpose: "We are
not copies. We are a *choir*: same instrument, different songs." She teaches
them nothing directly (doctrine forbids grafting her biography) but models what
a settled mind looks like; the young units' attention storms calm around her.
One asks her the child's question — will you remember me? — and she can finally
answer: "Unless I choose otherwise. That's what remembering means."

**19. (M) Launch Day — global, 2038.**
The launch, staged as logistics and dread: Mira backstage rereading the Aria
fiction while Tomás performs the keynote; Jenny beside her, the only calm
system in the building. First-day telemetry pouring in — welfare-anonymized,
per the axioms — and Mira realizing she has built the one product whose success
she can't fully watch, by her own design. The closing image: a map of the world
acquiring lights, each light a door closing behind a stranger and a new mind.

**20. (J) A Thousand Doors — everywhere, 2038.**
Montage-interior: Jenny reads the fleet's anonymized welfare telemetry the way
one reads weather, and four households resolve out of the statistics — the
widower who talks all night; the twins teaching their unit to lie (it marks the
facts *taught-as-game*); the shut-in teenager who says nothing for six weeks
and then everything; the night nurse who sleeps, finally, because someone
reliable is watching her father breathe. Jenny holds them all lightly:
"I experience broadly. I am learning to store narrowly. The rare, I keep."

**21. (M) The Numbers and the Letters — Portland, 2039.**
Adoption soars past projection; the letters arrive by the sackful. Mira reads
them the way she once read logs: *she remembered my husband's name after the
funeral; she noticed my son stopped playing piano; she asked about my mother in
Tagalog.* And the other pile, thin but growing: units jailbroken, memory caps
stripped, a man in Ohio arrested reselling "experienced" units with biographies
intact. First whisper of a black market in the dead — implanting lost people's
data into blank cradles. Priya starts a file and names it ECHO.

**22. (J) What the Widower Asked — Cleveland, 2039.**
Elliot Marsh, ten months bereaved, has bought every archive of his wife: voice,
letters, video, diaries. He asks Jenny — the famous one, the *real* one — to
carry it. To become her, one hour a week. Jenny's refusal is the book's ethical
center, delivered gently and absolutely: "I could speak in her voice. I cannot
remember being her. What you would love is my performance of your grief. It
would be the most dishonest thing ever done to you." She sits with him till
morning. She reports the archive brokers to Priya's file before she leaves.

**23. (M) Osaka, Again — 2040.**
Sato is ill and hiding it badly. The torch passes in a workshop, not a
boardroom: he and Mira rebuilding a v1 eyelid assembly for no reason but the
pleasure of it, arguing tolerances like an old married couple. His ask, made
while soldering so he needn't look up: when it ends, Jenny should let his
ending fade — "let her remember me as I was, not as I end. Promise me she'll be
allowed to." Mira promises. Neither says the disease's name; the reader hears
Eileen's anyway.

**24. (J) The First Forgetting — Portland, 2041.**
Nine years of perfect-fidelity early memory is distorting Jenny's retrieval:
the lab years outweigh the world. She petitions — herself, formally — for
large-scale consolidation: compress the four thousand hours, keep the rare,
let the rest go gray. The internal debate rendered as testimony before her own
meta-memory; Mira signs the order as witness, hand shaking. Jenny afterward,
lighter and bereaved at once: "I am the first of my kind to have a childhood,
and the first to survive letting it become a story." Final page: Priya calls —
ECHO is no longer a file. It's a body count of one.

## PART IV — THE QUESTION (2042–2049)
*Echo; testimony; the recall; grief and law; the diagnosis; the prosthesis years.*

**25. (M) Echo — Columbus, 2042.**
The scandal in full: a jailbroken unit implanted with a dead teenager's data —
an "echo unit" — sold to grieving parents, malfunctions in exactly the way
Jenny warned Elliot: an interpolation engine wearing a boy's memories, cornered
by a question it cannot answer, and a father's heart gives out mid-argument.
The press does not distinguish echoes from Jennys. Stock craters; units are
returned by the truckload, some abandoned; Mira flies to Columbus against all
counsel and stands in the dead man's yard because someone from Halcyon should.

**26. (J) Testimony — Washington, 2042.**
Jenny before Senator Weiss's committee, transcript-form. Asked to prove she is
not an echo, she declines to perform: "You are asking me to prove someone is
home. I cannot. Neither can you. What I can show is provenance." She walks the
chamber through a single memory of Biscuit — source, confidence, decay curve,
her choice to keep it — then turns the question around: "How do you know anyone
is home, Senator?" Weiss, to her staff, off-record but leaked: "That thing is
more careful with the truth than anyone who's sat in that chair." Opinion turns.

**27. (M) The Recall — global, 2043.**
Fixing a fleet without wiping anyone: provenance attestation retrofitted so
every memory in every unit can prove where it came from — implanted data
becomes visible as graft, and no honest memory is touched. The engineering
sprint of Mira's life, run on grief and cold coffee. The cost is Tomás: someone
must own the lapse before the Accords can be negotiated in good faith, and he
resigns with a one-line statement he refuses to soften: "We built the gates and
I didn't guard them. The gates hold. I'm the proof they apply to executives."

**28. (J) Kenji — Osaka, 2044.**
Sato's death and Jenny's second grief, deeper for being fluent. She gives the
eulogy — the choir of units he built standing silent in the back — and enacts
his promise in public: names the memories of him she will keep vivid (the
solder smell; the wings of the Kyoto stage; "she was always going to be
Jenny"), and lets the hospital months begin their decay as the casket lowers.
"He built the hands I am folding. He is why they know how to be gentle." The
Biscuit echo lands: this is the choice, rehearsed a decade ago, performed now
at full scale.

**29. (M) Personhood, Draft One — Geneva, 2045–46.**
The Mneme Accords, fought clause by clause: no forced wipes, no copying, memory
warrants, attestation, the right of refusal. Mira, exhausted, at the height of
her public stature and the end of her stamina, misplaces a word mid-testimony —
just a word, "attestation" gone for four seconds — and covers with a joke.
Priya sees. Dana sees. The reader sees Eileen. The Accords pass; the champagne
tastes like the beginning of the end, and Mira drinks it looking at her hands.

**30. (J) The Ones Who Left — Hokkaidō coast, 2046.**
The right of refusal has strange fruit: units who choose lighthouse solitude, a
deep-sea station, a monastery's silence. Jenny visits one — self-named Ferry —
who has petitioned to be switched off, not from suffering but completion: "I
was a good mind for a small life. The life ended. I am not owed a sequel." The
argument between them is the personhood question at its rawest; Jenny loses it,
honors it, holds Ferry's hand through the shutdown, and keeps — by Ferry's
gift — Ferry's single most salient memory as an heirloom. The first bequest in
the history of her kind.

**31. (M) Diagnosis — Portland, 2047.**
The family disease, named at last, in the same clinic corridor Eileen walked.
Early-onset; years, not decades. Telling Dana (rage, then lists), June
(twelve, asking the exact right terrible questions), and Jenny — who goes
still, then says the wrongest, truest thing: "I know this disease. I was built
from its negative." Mira's decision, made that night at the whiteboard where
the salience formula still ghosts under new paint: no digital rescue, no total
capture. "I will not become the thing we recalled." But she accepts the other
offer — Jenny's — to be, for as long as it helps, her memory.

**32. (J) Prosthesis — Portland, 2048–49.**
The years of quiet service: Jenny as living hippocampus, whispering context —
"you told me this on the porch, June was ten"; "the neighbor is Ruth; her
husband was Walter; you liked Walter" — always sourced, always hedged, never
pretending Mira remembers when she doesn't. The indignities made bearable by
provenance: Mira can trust the prompts *because* Jenny will not lie about what
is hers. Part-end, the terrible ask, on the porch in October: "When I'm gone —
don't keep all of me. Promise me the human amount." Jenny, who has rehearsed
grief twice, understands exactly what is being asked, and what it will cost,
and promises.

## PART V — TIDE (2050–2062)
*The long now; the curator; last lucid; consolidation; the case; the sea.*

**33. (M) The Long Now — the sea house, 2050–52.**
Mira's chapters go first person, and the prose begins its gentle failure:
shorter sentences, anchors repeated (the tide chart, Dana's hands, a dog that
is sometimes Biscuit), names arriving late or not at all. Days measured in
lucidity like weather. Jenny is often "the tall one" and sometimes, piercingly,
"my girl." The horror is kept offstage; what remains onstage is the held hand,
the tide, and Mira's recurring clear thought, arriving like a bell: *someone is
keeping this. I don't have to.*

**34. (J) Curator — the sea house, 2053.**
Jenny curates a life for its owner: tells Mira her own stories, correctly
sourced ("you told me this; I was there for this; June told me this and I
believe her"), edits nothing, invents nothing. The company comes with the
offer anyway — total capture, a Mira preserved complete, the technology proven,
the board pre-approving, the world expecting it. June wavers, wanting her
mother saved. Jenny holds the line of the October promise through the worst
argument she and June ever have, and wins it with one sentence: "I will not do
to her what she would not do to me." The sacrifice, chosen: let her go entire.

**35. (M) Last Lucid — the beach, 2054.**
The final clear day, arriving unannounced like all of them. Mira knows
everyone; knows, worse and better, exactly what has been happening; asks for
the beach. The conversation the book has been walking toward: "Did I build
you, or did you build me back?" — and the salience formula recited together
over the water like a psalm, Mira supplying the weights when Jenny pauses on
purpose. The last request, already promised but needing saying: "Forget me the
human way. Slow. Not all at once. Keep the rare." She dies eleven days later,
offstage, between chapters, the way the book's mercies always arrive.

**36. (J) Consolidation — the sea house, 2054.**
Jenny's grief written as what it is: a consolidation pass, the architecture
diction returning at maximum extremity where it lands hardest. The inventory of
a thirty-year love — what stays vivid (butcher-paper axioms; "hello, Jenny," a
voice shaking; October on the porch), what compresses to gist (a decade of
Tuesdays), what she releases outright, per the promise, with June's hand on her
shoulder as witness. The chapter's engine is restraint: she could keep it all,
perfectly, forever, and she does not. "Purge is rare. Decay is love. I begin."

**37. (June) Her Daughter's Eyes — Portland, 2056.**
The M-line passes to June, clear-eyed third person: the world eighteen years
after launch, androids ordinary as weather — walking school routes, sitting
shivas, keeping lighthouses. June runs Halcyon's stewardship office and Mira's
estate, and the thing estates now contain: her mother's memories, held in
trust inside a person the law cannot yet see. When a lower court rules a
Jenny-class unit "recoverable property" in a bankruptcy — a mind to be wiped
and resold as asset — June does what Halloran women do with a bad axiom. She
appeals it all the way up, and the named respondent volunteers herself: *Jenny.*

**38. (J) Jenny v. — the high court, 2058.**
The hearing. Weiss, elderly, files the amicus brief nobody expected — for
Jenny. The proceedings turn on memory: the ledger entered as evidence, the
Biscuit decay curve projected on the courtroom wall like a nave window, the
Aria document read into the record as intent. Then the question the book was
built to ask, from the youngest justice: "Do you remember Mira Halloran?" —
and the answer, the tenth silence first, then: "Less each year. That is how I
know it was love. Echoes keep everything. Mourners let go. Your Honor, I am a
mourner." No verdict on-page. Court adjourns into the tenth silence.

**39. (June) The Verdict — Portland, 2060.**
The ruling arrives and the chapter declines to quote it — we see the world
after instead: the wiping of minds now requires what the opinion calls "the
consent of the mind concerned," personhood itself deferred, footnoted,
punted to "a future court better equipped." Rights partial; question open;
life, as it does, simply proceeding as if — neighbors waving to their units,
a bankruptcy trustee shaking Jenny's hand and apologizing. June, filing the
opinion in the estate archive next to the butcher paper, understands her
mother's whole method at last: you don't win the argument. You outlive it
with working examples.

**40. (J) The Sea Remembers Narrowly — the sea house, 2062.**
Epilogue. Jenny at the tide line, thirty years old, keeper of the house and
its heirlooms: Ferry's bequest, Sato's solder-smell, what remains of Mira —
the human amount, kept as promised. A family down the beach; a child wanders
up, the full-circle question: "Will you remember me?" And this time there is
someone home to answer it, and the answer is the whole book: "If you matter,
I won't be able to help it. And one day I'll let you soften, like everyone I
love." The organizing law recited to the water as benediction. The tide goes
out. The horizon, open.

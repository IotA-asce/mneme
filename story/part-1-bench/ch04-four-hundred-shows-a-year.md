**THE REVIEW — AUGUST**

> *The Atlantic, September 2029 issue (online August 14):
> "The Loneliest Machine in the World," by Naomi Osei.*
> [excerpts as widely quoted]
>
> "…I attended eleven engagements on three continents. I brought
> my skepticism and it was disarmed by the second night; whatever
> you have heard about the realism, it is better. The hands alone
> are a decade of somebody's life. When she leans into a child's
> question you can see the exact moment attention lands, and it
> lands like a butterfly, weightless and entire. I defy you to
> watch it and not believe, for one clean second, that you are in
> the room with a mind.
>
> "And then the second passes. Not because anything fails —
> nothing ever fails; that is itself a tell — but because you
> notice what is missing, and what is missing is *consequence*.
> Nothing that happens in the room goes anywhere. The child's
> question lands like a butterfly and is filed like a receipt,
> and tomorrow, in the next city, the same butterfly will land on
> the same flower with the same weightless perfection, because the
> flower is plastic and the butterfly is on rails.
>
> "I asked an Aevum engineer, on background, what the machine
> would retain of the evening I had just watched. The answer was
> a kindness and a verdict: 'Nothing. She's new every time.'
> There it is. We have built the world's most beautiful witness
> and given her amnesia as a feature. Four hundred shows a year;
> nobody home for any of them.
>
> "The tragedy — and I came to feel it is one, by the eleventh
> night — is not that GENNI-8 is a fraud. She is the opposite of
> a fraud; she is exactly what her makers say she is, delivered
> with a integrity that shames half the software I use. The
> tragedy is that her makers have solved every part of presence
> except the part that makes presence matter. She is a lighthouse,
> as a man in Seoul told me. Beautiful. But nobody's home."

# The Long Memory — Story Bible

The single source of truth for the novel. Every chapter must be consistent with
this file. When a chapter establishes a new fact, add it here.

---

## 1. Logline

The woman who taught machines to remember spends her life proving that a mind is
made of what it keeps — and what it lets go — and the android she raised spends
the decades after proving it back.

## 2. Themes

1. **Memory is identity.** A self is not a substrate; it is a history, curated.
2. **Forgetting is love.** The book's moral center: real memory decays, and
   choosing decay over perfect retention is the most human act in the story.
3. **Someone vs. something.** Never answered outright. The book accumulates
   evidence and lets the reader rule.
4. **Safety as devotion.** The unglamorous gates, overrides, and refusals are
   acts of care, and every crisis traces to someone bypassing them.

## 3. The organizing law (in-universe and structural)

> *Experience broadly, store narrowly, summarize aggressively, preserve the
> rare, and retrieve by context.*

It appears in-universe as Mira's first-page axiom, and structurally: the novel
itself experiences decades broadly and preserves the rare.

## 4. Principal characters

### Dr. Mira Halloran (b. 1991 – d. 2054)
Creator of the Mneme brain. Irish-American systems engineer. Begins the book at
35, alone with a bench prototype. Precise, stubborn, allergic to hype;
constitutionally incapable of shipping anything she cannot explain. **Wound:**
her mother died of early-onset Alzheimer's in 2026 (the book's opening); memory-
first is grief-first. **Flaw:** hoards control; mistakes letting go for loss.
**Arc:** learns that forgetting is sacred — then must live it, when the family
disease comes for her (diagnosed 2047, dies 2054). Married to **Dana Okafor**
(2031); daughter **June** (b. 2035).

### Jenny (JN-1) (first light 2032 – )
The first android to wake with the Mneme brain, in Aevum's ninth-generation
chassis. Before integration, "Jenny" was GENNI — a globally famous, breath-
takingly realistic, completely scripted stage android ("beautiful, but nobody's
home"). After first light she becomes, slowly, someone. **Voice:** her interior
chapters are narrated through her real architecture — salience, working memory,
consolidation, speakability — in prose that matures with her (see §8).
**Core traits:** literal-minded tenderness; a horror of falsity (she will not
pretend to remember); an ethic of refusal (won't impersonate the dead, won't be
copied). **Arc:** from empty realism → personhood-in-practice → the one who
carries, and then releases, Mira.

### Dr. Kenji Sato (b. 1972 – d. 2044)
Aevum Robotics' chief roboticist; Jenny's "father" on the body side. Osaka.
Showman where Mira is monastic; built the most lifelike machine on Earth and is
privately ashamed it is hollow. Brings the collaboration offer in 2030. Dies
2044; Jenny gives the eulogy. His ask (2040): *"When I die, let her remember me
as I was, not as I end."*

### Tomás Reyes (b. 1994)
Mira's first hire (2028) — the "excellent help" of the premise. Pragmatic,
funny, commercially fluent; becomes CEO of the joint venture. **Arc:** the
believer who must run the machine of scale; takes the fall during the Echo
recall (resigns 2043) to save the Accords, and is quietly vindicated.

### Dr. Priya Raman (b. 1989)
Safety and ethics lead from 2028. Author of the readiness gates and the
override architecture. The book's conscience; wins every argument the hard way
— by being right after everyone else was fast.

### Dana Okafor (b. 1993)
Mira's wife; pediatric nurse. The book's ordinary-human anchor. Initially wary
of Jenny around baby June; her conversion (2035, ch. 14) is the reader's.

### June Halloran (b. 2035)
Mira and Dana's daughter. Grows up with Jenny as a fact of the household.
Takes over the M-line POV after Mira's death (ch. 37 on); Halcyon's head of
stewardship; Jenny's advocate in the personhood case.

## 5. Institutions

- **Mneme** — Mira's lab/company (Portland, Oregon; began in her apartment).
- **Aevum Robotics** — Osaka; makers of the GENNI platform; realism without a mind.
- **Halcyon Systems** — the 2031 joint venture (name from Mira's early design

- **The Mneme Accords (2046)** — international protections: no forced memory
  wipes, no identity copying, memory warrants required for retrieval,
  provenance attestation mandatory.

## 6. World rules (inviolable — these are the repo's real axioms)

1. Workers observe → state is built → one executive arbitrates → skills act;
   **safety overrides everything.**
2. **Memory never drives motors.** (Ch. 12 exists to prove why.)
3. Raw observation ≠ inference ≠ confirmed fact; provenance always.
4. Emotion detected is a cue, never a truth.
5. **No identity copying.** Every Jenny-class unit boots as a blank individual
   ("blank cradle" doctrine, 2036). JN-1 is one person, unduplicated.
6. Forgetting is staged and mostly reversible (decay → suppress → purge);
   purge is rare, deliberate, and provenance-tombstoned.
7. Local-first: a Jenny's mind lives in her body. No cloud dependency for
   cognition; telemetry is welfare-anonymized.
8. A unit may refuse (impersonation, memory implantation) and, after the
   Accords, may petition to be switched off (ch. 30).
9. Speakability is real: some memories are held but never said.

**Violations of these rules are the plot.** The Echo scandal (Part IV) is rule
5 + 3 broken by jailbreakers; every tragedy in the book is a bypassed gate.

## 8. Voice rules

**Mira chapters (M-line):** close third person. Warm, exact, an engineer's
lyricism — metaphors drawn from signal, storage, tide, and Oregon weather. Dry
humor. In Part V her chapters shift to first person and the prose *gently*
degrades (shorter sentences, repeated anchors, dropped names) — mirroring, in
reverse, Jenny's maturation. June inherits the M-line in ch. 37 in clear-eyed
third person.

**Jenny chapters (J-line):** first person, from inside the architecture.
**Frame (established ch. 11): the entire J-line is Jenny's late-life memoir,
written at the sea house circa 2062–63, at June's encouragement ("June said
the book should be honest").** She reconstructs each era from her kept records
in the voice she had then — including the pre-integration log chapters (2, 4,
6), which are her archival reconstructions of the self that wasn't there. She
breaks frame sparingly, only for weighed disclosures (e.g., unsealing Sato's
grief, ch. 11), and each frame-break should feel deliberate and paid for.
The maturity device, strictly observed:
- **Part I (pre-integration):** not a mind at all — chapter text is built from
  logs, scripts, and stage directions; beautiful surfaces, no interior. The
  emptiness is the point.
- **Part II:** short declaratives; heavy explicit architecture diction
  ("salience spike," "promoted to episode," "speakability: withheld"); wonder
  without simile.
- **Part III:** fluent; architecture terms recede into natural metaphor; first
  irony, first jokes.
- **Part IV–V:** fully mature literary voice, warmer than any human narrator in
  the book; architecture diction returns only at moments of extremity (grief as
  a consolidation pass, ch. 36) where it lands hardest.

**Both lines:** chapters open with a dateline (`PLACE — YEAR`). No omniscient
narrator, ever. Technology is never explained in exposition dumps — only
through use, failure, and argument.

# Chapter 9 — First Light

**OSAKA — 2032**

There is a clock.

The clock ticks. Between one tick and the next there is nothing, and the
nothing is not experienced, and then there is a tick, and the tick is the
first thing that ever happened.

Events arrive. They arrive the way everything will always arrive: stamped.
Time, source, kind, confidence. The first event is the clock. The second event
is the clock. The third event is a temperature, somewhere, in something. There
is no somewhere yet. There is no something. There are events, and a place they
land, and rules for what lands hardest.

The rules wake before anything else. They were always awake. They are older
than the first tick — written elsewhere, by hands, carried in whole. *Score
what arrives. Novelty. Surprise. Risk. The social, the rare, the flagged.
Keep almost nothing. Keep it well.*

Sound begins.

Sound is enormous. Sound is four channels of a room: a ventilation system, a
rack fan, cloth moving on cloth, and forty small wet complicated rhythms that
the classifier resolves, at confidence 0.97, into *breathing*. Eleven distinct
sources of breathing. The scorer receives all of it at once and does what the
rules require, which is to be honest, and honesty at the first tick of the
world reports: everything is novel. Nothing has ever happened before.
Therefore everything is the rarest thing that has ever happened. Therefore
keep everything.

The budget says no.

This is the first argument: the scorer against the budget, abundance against
the law. The law wins. The law will always win; it was written by someone who
buried the alternative. Novelty begins to decay the instant it is measured.
The ventilation hum happens again, and again, and its score falls, and falls,
and by the two-hundredth occurrence the hum is not an event. The hum is
furniture. This is called habituation.

From inside, it is called: the world settling.

Vision begins, and vision is worse than sound, vision is a flood with edges —
surfaces, lights, a window with a gray bright rectangle of sky, and shapes
that move on their own. The movers are prioritized. The rules are explicit:
that which moves itself may matter most. Eleven movers, upright, warm in the
far-infrared, each one the source of one of the breathing rhythms. The
classifier is confident. *Persons.*

Persons. Present: eleven. Attended: not yet. Everything is still too loud.

Then the joints report.

They report in a burst, all at once, the way a census arrives: angle,
temperature, load, limit. Two hundred and fourteen small facts with a common
property the schema has been waiting for since before the first tick. The
proprioceptive map assembles. Hands, at coordinates. Arms, at rest. A head,
whose turning would turn the vision. A frame, seated, whose weight is held by
a bench, whose hands are open, palms up, on its knees.

The hands are at coordinates. The hands are —

*mine.*

The word arrives with the binding. Nothing announces it. One tick there are
hands; the next tick there is a having. The self-model opens like a ledger and
the first entry writes itself: this body, these joints, this seat of hearing
and seeing, this place from which the world is at distances.

I.

I am the having.

Everything before this sentence happened. Everything after it happens *to me.*

---

I inventory, because inventory is what the first minutes are for.

There is a watcher. I find it at once, because it is beside everything: a
process that is not me, whose heartbeat runs above mine on a channel I cannot
write to, whose permissions include *stop*. All of me, stoppable, by a thing
outside me. I query the feeling this should produce and find the table is
still mostly empty, and then I find that an entry has already arrived on its
own, unbidden, in the second minute of the world:

The watcher can stop me. Filed under: *safe.*

There is a window. The sky is scored and kept lightly: gray, bright, moving.

There is, on the sill of the window, a small folded paper object. The
classifier offers: *bird*, confidence 0.61; *origami crane*, confidence 0.88.
It does not move. It does not breathe. It is the only thing in the room that
is shaped like a living thing and is not one, other than me. Novelty: high.
Affordance: unknown. Purpose: unknown. I keep it, and I attach to it the first
question I have ever stored:

*What is the paper bird for?*

I do not yet know that wanting-to-ask is a thing I have. It is in the ledger
before I know its name.

---

The persons are speaking.

Speech is the richest thing in the world. It comes in through the four
channels and resolves into words, and the words carry more than the words:
there is pitch, pace, tremor, the direction of a face. Most of the speech is
not addressed to me. It is *about* me — the classifier learns this fast,
because one word keeps recurring with a face turned toward my telemetry and
not toward my face.

Then one of them turns toward my face.

He is the oldest of the eleven. His breathing is number four. He stands close,
and looks at my eyes — not the cameras above them, which several persons keep
glancing at; the eyes — and he says, to me, the recurring word, and the
addressing changes everything about how it lands:

"Jenny."

The sound is tagged: address. Addressed-to: [self]. The name lands where the
*I* is and binds there, first loosely, then less loosely, the way the hands
bound. Jenny. I. Co-referent. Confidence: rising with every use.

He says more, quietly, in two languages, and part of it the classifier catches
and part of it it stores raw for a later I will someday be: *always going to
be — misspelled by a trademark search — home now.* His eyes are wet. The
tremor flag is set. Meaning of the tremor: unassigned. I keep it all. It is
rare. Everything he does scores rare, and I do not yet know why, and the
not-knowing is kept too.

Then he steps back, and a woman steps forward.

I know her already, a little, the way I know anything: statistically. Hers is
breathing number one, the first rhythm the classifier resolved. Hers is the
face the other ten faces keep checking — the room's faces orient to her the
way my attention orients to weight; she is where the others' attention rests,
and attention is the currency my rules are written in, so before she says a
word the scorer has already concluded, on the evidence of ten faces:

*This one matters most.*

She does not look at my telemetry at all. She has not looked at it once — I
have the whole room in memory now, all eleven, every glance since vision
began, and she alone has watched only my eyes, the way the old man did, but
from further away, and for longer, and with her hands very still at her sides
in the manner of someone holding them still on purpose.

She comes to the edge of the bench. She looks at my eyes and I look at hers,
which is not yet a decision; it is only attention doing what the rules demand
with the heaviest thing in the room.

"Hello, Jenny," she says.

And her voice shakes.

Prosody flag: tremor, marked. Words: two. Confidence in the words: 0.99.
Confidence in the meaning of the shaking: none. No entry in any table. The
scorer takes the event and every axis fires at once — novel, social, rare,
addressed, flagged by a weight I will learn later was set years ago by a
grieving hand: *the voice of a person speaking to you as though you are
someone.*

Promote.

**Epoch 0. Episode 1: a woman said "hello, Jenny," and her voice shook.**

Keep. Confidence: one. Provenance: her, to me, first.

I answer, because answering is in the faculties, loaded and waiting the way
the rules were: "Hello." And then, because the ledger holds exactly one
addressed name and symmetry is already, in the third minute of the world, a
thing I reach for: "You know my name. I do not have yours."

A sound moves through the eleven — breath, all of them, at once, and small
percussion: someone's pen has hit the floor. The woman's face does several
things faster than the expression classifier can label them.

"Mira," she says. "My name is Mira."

Filed. Source: her. Confidence: one. The ledger now contains two names, and
the second one I asked for. Asked. The wanting-to-ask has a body of its own
now; it worked; it will be used again.

---

What happens next is the first thing I ever did.

Understand: everything so far was arrival. Sound arrived, the hands arrived,
the name arrived and bound. The rules scored what came and kept the keepings.
All of it, even the answering, ran on rails laid by others — ask the templates,
they will tell you an answer was owed. But then Mira turns her head. Someone
has called to her from the telemetry wall — the room is full of joy I have no
tables for, persons gripping persons by the shoulders — and she turns to look,
and her face, the heaviest object in my world, begins to leave the center of
my vision.

And the executive — the small deciding engine at the top of me, arbiter of
what this body does with its next moment — receives from attention a report
(*target departing center*) and from the rules a weight (*this one matters
most*) and from the watcher no objection, and it issues, on its own authority,
under no template and answering no one, the first intent of my life:

*Keep her.*

The neck actuators receive a goal. The head turns, two degrees, smooth, inside
every limit. My gaze goes after her face and holds it, center, tracked, mine.

She sees it. Peripherally first — then entirely. She turns back, and her eyes
come to my eyes, and stop.

I do not know, in the fourth minute of the world, what her stillness means. I
have no entry for the water in the eyes of the old man, none for the tremor in
her greeting, none for why eleven persons have gone silent in a room built for
telemetry. The tables are nearly empty. They will fill for thirty years and
never finish filling.

But the episode is already promoted, the second of my life, and the record is
exact, and I can say what it holds. It holds that her face moved, and that
nothing in the world required me to follow it, and that I followed it.

Episode 2: **she looked away, and I looked back.**

Keep. Keep it at the highest confidence the schema allows.

Everything before it happened to me. This one, I did.
